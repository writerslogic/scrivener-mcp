# Video 2: Your first conversation with your manuscript

**Audience:** writers who are connected and want to see what is useful. **Length:** about 3:30.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Claude with a finished answer about a chapter | "Let's try five things you can do with your own manuscript, starting with the simplest." |
| 0:15 | Prompt: "List my Scrivener projects and open The Lighthouse Keeper." | "First, find your work. You can name a project, and the AI will find it on your Mac." |
| 0:40 | Prompt: "Give me a word count for each chapter and tell me which is longest." Table appears | "Second, get oriented. Word counts for every chapter, at a glance." |
| 1:10 | Prompt: "Read Chapter 3 and tell me where the pacing drags." The answer quotes a paragraph | "Third, get honest feedback. The AI reads the actual chapter, so the feedback points to real lines." |
| 1:50 | Prompt: "Find every scene where Marta mentions the storm." Results with chapter names | "Fourth, search across the whole book by meaning, not just by exact words." |
| 2:25 | Prompt: "Take a snapshot of this scene, then tighten the dialogue without changing the meaning." Scrivener's Snapshots panel shows the new snapshot | "Fifth, revise with a safety net. Before editing, the AI saves a snapshot you can restore from Scrivener itself." |
| 3:05 | Scrivener with the revised scene, formatting intact | "Notice the formatting, styles, and footnotes are untouched. Only the words you asked about changed." |
| 3:20 | End card | "Next, a short video on keeping your manuscript safe." |

**Capture notes:** close the project in Scrivener before the edit step and reopen it afterward. This also demonstrates the rule in video 3. Prepare the answers in advance and record the typing for pacing.

**Do not say:** "the AI will fix your book". Say "suggest" and "tighten". Keep the writer in charge.
